======================================================================
1. THE STORY (what the paper claims)
======================================================================
One-sentence pitch:
  We predict how hard a generated level is from its layout alone, in
  milliseconds and without running anything, and we certify that the
  difficulty labels are repeatable before we try to predict them.
  This works where difficulty is spatial, which covers most of today's
  procedurally generated RL benchmarks; it does not work where difficulty
  is logical, and we say so.

Three candidate story shapes (pick after stage 1 + stage 2 results):

  STORY A (preferred if curriculum results are positive)
    Part 1  Reliability gate: labels must be repeatable before prediction.
    Part 2  Cheap spatial difficulty prediction beats every execution-free
            baseline and matches up to 16 rollouts of a trained agent.
    Part 3  Use case: grader-ordered curriculum and SFL+grader that
            replaces most of SFL's scoring rollouts.

  STORY B (if curriculum results are null or weak)
    Parts 1 and 2 as above are the paper.
    Curriculum becomes a short "first use" section or a limitation,
    reported honestly (pre-registered, null result stated as such).

  STORY C (fallback emphasis if reviewers would see prediction as thin)
    Lead with measurement: most single-run difficulty labels are noise
    (1-35% signal; 4 to >140 seeds needed for 0.7 reliability; 5 of 12
    Karel tasks fail the bar; an old PPO oracle had 91% tied labels).
    Prediction is then "what you can do once labels are trustworthy".

Decision rule: stage 1 key line is "grader vs random_curriculum";
stage 2 key line is "sfl_grader vs sfl_small". p < 0.05 on either
supports Story A. Neither -> Story B.

======================================================================
2. SPATIAL VS LOGICAL: FRAMING
======================================================================
Definitions (apply the same test to every task/benchmark):
  SPATIAL  The level generator varies the layout (walls, obstacles,
           paths, box/goal positions); the goal and rules stay fixed.
           Difficulty follows from the layout's shape.
  LOGICAL  The generator varies rules, goals, dependency chains or the
           program needed to solve the task; the layout matters little.
           Difficulty is in the sequence of steps.
  OUT OF SCOPE  Physics/dynamics benchmarks (e.g. BipedalWalker terrain,
           Kinetix) where difficulty comes from contact, balance and
           momentum. Mention once in the paper as outside our scope; do
           not classify or claim them.

Claim to make (coverage, not superiority):
  "Most procedurally generated curriculum / environment-design
   benchmarks vary the layout. That is the regime our predictor covers."
  Back it with the verified survey table (section 4).

Why spatial matters in practice (our motivation paragraph):
  - Layout generators are cheap, so they produce huge amounts of
    synthetic training levels; this is why curriculum methods (PLR,
    ACCEL, SFL) are built and tested on them. A cheap difficulty
    signal is most useful exactly where levels are abundant.
  - Logical task generation is often more costly to design and tends
    to target a specific scenario. CAVEAT (verified): XLand-MiniGrid does
    generate logical tasks at scale, sampled from a precomputed bank of
    rule sets. So do NOT claim "logical tasks can't be generated"; claim
    only coverage.

Evidence we have for the split:
  - Spatial (Sokoban, LavaGap, LavaCrossing): our grader is the best
    execution-free method in every case (section 3).
  - Logical (most Karel tasks): our features fail (rho near 0); an LLM
    judge does somewhat better but nothing does well (most rho < 0.3,
    error bars ~ +/-0.13).
  - Karel tasks that are spatial in nature (WallAvoider, TopOff,
    OneStroke) are where our features still work. This supports the
    split rather than being an exception.

STATUS: VERIFIED 2026-10-02 against each paper's full text / official repo
(all arXiv IDs matched titles). Quotes are verbatim from the fetched text.

  Procgen Benchmark (Cobbe et al. 2019/2020, ICML; arXiv 1912.01588)
    Varies: per-seed layout, assets, entity locations and spawn timing.
      "level layout..., the selection of game assets, the location and
       spawn times of entities, and other game-specific details" (Sec 2)
    Per game: Maze/Heist/Chaser = Kruskal mazes (Heist adds locks/keys);
      CaveFlyer/Miner/Dodgeball = layouts; CoinRun/Jumper/Climber/Ninja =
      platform layouts + jump timing; BigFish/Leaper/StarPilot/Plunder/
      Bossfight = spawn timing and reactive play. No game varies rules.
    Category: SPATIAL overall, but HONEST CAVEAT: the timing/reactive
      games are not readable from a static layout; our method covers the
      maze-like subset, partially the platformers, not the reactive ones.

  PLR: Prioritized Level Replay (Jiang et al. 2020, ICML 2021; 2010.03934)
    Environments: all 16 Procgen games + MiniGrid MultiRoom-N4-Random,
      ObstructedMazeGamut-Easy/Medium.
    Varies: MultiRoom = room count and positions; ObstructedMaze tiers add
      hidden keys, blocking balls, extra locked rooms.
      "further requires the agent to first uncover the key from under a box
       before picking up the key" (App A.2)
    Category: Procgen, MultiRoom SPATIAL; ObstructedMaze MIXED (fixed rules,
      longer subgoal chain).

  PAIRED (Dennis et al. 2020, NeurIPS; 2012.02096)
    Environments: 15x15 MiniGrid navigation; MuJoCo hopper.
    Varies: adversary places agent, goal, then up to 50 walls.
      "at timestep 0 it places the agent, 1 the goal, and every step
       afterwards an obstacle" (Sec 5.1)
    Category: grid SPATIAL; hopper OUT OF SCOPE (physics).
    Note: no CarRacing here (that is Robust PLR, Jiang et al. 2021a; not
      checked).

  ACCEL (Parker-Holder et al. 2022, ICML; 2203.01302)
    Environments: MiniGrid mazes, MiniHack 7x7 lava grid, BipedalWalker.
      No CarRacing.
    Varies: edits add/remove wall blocks and move the goal (mazes),
      add/remove lava (MiniHack), step 8 terrain parameters (Bipedal).
      "we begin with empty rooms and randomly edit the block locations (by
       adding or removing blocks), as well as the goal location" (Sec 4.1)
    Category: mazes/lava SPATIAL; BipedalWalker OUT OF SCOPE.

  SFL / "No Regrets" (Rutherford et al. 2024; 2408.15099; venue not stated
    in fetched text)
    Environments: JaxNav (single and multi-robot), MiniGrid, XLand-MiniGrid.
    Varies (JaxNav): 11x11 m map with up to 60% wall fill, start and goal
      positions; differential-drive robot with LiDAR.
      "involves low-level continuous control obstacle avoidance in addition
       to maze solving" (App I.1)
    Category: JaxNav SPATIAL (+ kinematic control); MiniGrid SPATIAL;
      XLand-MiniGrid LOGICAL. Paper notes ACCEL editing does not apply to
      XLand (levels come from a precomputed ruleset bank).

  XLand-MiniGrid (Nikulin et al. 2023, NeurIPS 2024 D&B; 2312.12044)
    Varies: ruleset = goal + tree of production rules (depth 0-3) +
      distractor rules; object/door positions. Room grid fixed per env.
      "Positions of the doors, as well as their colors, and objects are
       randomized between resets, but the grid itself does not change."
       (App I)
    Category: LOGICAL. Confirms logical tasks CAN be generated at scale
      (from a precomputed ruleset bank).

  Craftax (Matthews et al. 2024, ICML; 2402.16801)
    Varies: 9 floors via Perlin noise + room generation (terrain, resources,
      creatures); potion effects permuted per episode. Crafting tree and
      achievements are fixed.
      "Each floor contains distinct challenges in the forms of different
       terrain generation, enemies and required skills" (Sec 3.2)
    Category: MIXED. Generator varies mostly layout; difficulty is mostly
      the fixed long-horizon tech tree + survival/combat.

  MiniHack (Samvelyan et al. 2021, NeurIPS D&B; 2109.13202)
    Varies: des-file layouts (mazes, corridors), monsters, objects, terrain;
      skill tasks sample which tool/item is needed.
      "distributions of grid layouts together with monsters, objects on the
       floor, environment features (e.g. walls, water, lava), etc." (3.1)
    Category: MIXED across the suite; navigation + UED demo SPATIAL, skill
      tasks lean LOGICAL.

  BabyAI (Chevalier-Boisvert et al. 2018, ICLR 2019; 1810.08272)
    Varies: grammar-generated instruction + room/maze layout and objects;
      19 levels adding competencies (GoTo, Put, Seq, Unlock, Maze...).
      "a level is a distribution of missions, where a mission combines an
       instruction within an initial environment state" (3.3)
    Category: MIXED (instructions logical, layouts spatial).

  JaxUED library (Coward, Beukman, Foerster 2024; arXiv 2403.13091 per
    README; github.com/DramaCow/jaxued)
    Ships: MiniGrid-style Maze (core), examples for Craftax and gymnax
      Acrobot/Pendulum/Cartpole; DR, PLR, Robust PLR, ACCEL, PAIRED.
      "a LevelSampler, a general environment interface UnderspecifiedEnv,
       and a Maze implementation" (README)
    Category: Maze SPATIAL; gymnax examples OUT OF SCOPE.

  Kinetix (Matthews et al. 2024, ICLR 2025; 2410.23208)
    "procedurally generating tens of millions of 2D physics-based tasks"
    Category: OUT OF SCOPE (physics). Only abstract checked.

  Karel / LEAPS (our own domain): program synthesis. LOGICAL.

Summary for the paper (about 13 environment families):
  SPATIAL ~5-6 (MiniGrid mazes/PAIRED grid, MultiRoom, MiniHack lava/nav,
  JaxNav, JaxUED Maze, most Procgen), MIXED ~4 (BabyAI, ObstructedMaze,
  Craftax, MiniHack skills), physics ~3-4 (out of scope), LOGICAL 1
  (XLand-MiniGrid).
  Key sentence: the canonical UED testbed (PAIRED -> ACCEL -> SFL ->
  JaxUED) is the MiniGrid maze, where the generator only moves walls,
  goal and agent, i.e. exactly the spatial regime we cover.
  Honest limits: Procgen's reactive/timing games and the logical parts of
  mixed suites are not covered; Robust PLR's CarRacing not checked.
